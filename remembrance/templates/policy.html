THE CARE BEHIND REMEMBRANCE

Memory deserves care.

You choose what you share. Visitors never pay to remember someone. There are no ads or third-party tracking scripts.

Consent comes first.

By creating an account, you agree to use Remembrance only for material you are authorized to preserve and share. You retain your rights and grant the operator a limited, nonexclusive permission to store, back up, and display what you upload to your selected audience. You may archive content or request erasure. Do not upload unlawful content, expose sensitive information about living people without a lawful basis, impersonate an estate representative, or violate someone else’s copyright.

Creating a memorial records your authority declaration, typed name, and time of consent. It does not independently verify legal authority. A family relationship alone does not establish ownership of every photo, recording, account, or message. Known wishes, disputes, and rights of living people require separate consideration.

Who can see a memory?

Public memorials are open to anyone with the link. Family memorials require an account granted access by the owner. Private memorials are visible only to their owner. Each media item and timeline chapter also has a visibility setting. Server operators with database or backup access can access stored data; this service is not end-to-end encrypted.

Reviewed sharing.

Owners can add a sharing grant that requires operator review, and can choose to hold sharing until passing is separately confirmed. Recording a grant pauses visitor access until its conditions are met. Revoking it blocks future visitor access while the owner retains their memories and account access. Reviewed sharing covers the memorial’s current and future original content within its visibility settings; it does not authorize voice synthesis, generated messages, advertising or model training.

When enabled, the server stores the typed declaration, grant status and release settings, review and confirmation timestamps, operator identifiers, evidence-reference digests, and a chained record of consent decisions. Evidence documents are handled separately by the operator. The app records human review decisions and does not independently verify legal authority.

What this server stores.

Account names, email addresses, password hashes, memorial information, uploaded media, consent declarations, family access lists, successor preferences, erasure requests, tributes, reports, and a record of changes are stored on this server. Image uploads are resized and rewritten to remove embedded metadata; video and audio metadata may remain. Upload only information you intend to store here.

Essential cookies maintain your signed-in session, protect forms, and avoid repeat candle entries in the same browser on the same day. Candle identifiers expire after 30 days. Abuse protection uses keyed hashes of network addresses, not a visitor analytics profile. The hosting provider and reverse proxy may maintain operational logs under the operator’s retention policy.

Your words remain your choice.

Tributes wait for owner approval. Keep the withdrawal code shown at submission to erase your name and tribute from the live database. Archiving other content hides it but does not erase it. Contact the operator for access, correction, account deletion, permanent erasure, or an ownership dispute. Public content can be copied by others, and previously downloaded archives cannot be remotely recalled.

Passing on memorial care.

An owner can nominate a confirmed account to care for a memorial. The invitation shares the memorial’s name and the owner’s account name with that account. The nominee’s response, typed declaration and review history are stored. Acceptance adds no viewing or editing access. An operator must independently review authority before transferring care.

After transfer, the new owner can manage and export the complete memorial, including private content and its consent records. The former owner’s management access and recorded family membership end. The memorial’s address and existing sharing controls remain in place. Nomination and acceptance can be withdrawn before transfer; a later transfer requires a new reviewed process.

Permanent erasure.

An owner can request permanent erasure from memorial care. Recording the request archives the memorial immediately. An operator reviews authority, disputes and legal holds before erasing it, and may decline. Erasure removes the memorial’s content, stored media files, tributes, candles, family access, reports, sharing grants, nominations and activity record from the live database.

A tamper-evident consent history keeps opaque identifiers, times, fixed event codes and digests, without names, stories or media. The operator keeps a separate erasure ledger of memorial identifiers and review-reference digests so that restoring a backup cannot bring an erased memorial back. Backups expire on the operator’s retention schedule, and downloaded or shared copies cannot be recalled.

Portability and continuity.

Owners can download a ZIP with stored media, structured JSON, and an offline HTML viewer. Hosting and domain renewal still require a responsible operator. Remembrance makes no contractual promise of perpetual hosting, a fixed outdoor plaque lifetime, or a funded escrow arrangement. Keep your own backups and a successor plan.
